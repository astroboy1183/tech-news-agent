The one structural change

Phase 1 now ends with an ugly, real front page

The layout meets a real headline on day 4, not day 14

The earlier plan built the website at Phase 4. That meant the design would not touch live data for two weeks — and real headlines are ragged: 24 to 190 characters, colons, CVE numbers, appended outlet names, ALL-CAPS. Every front page that has ever broken, broke there.

So Phase 1 now ends with /raw — the composition slots filled with live feed data and no CSS at all. It exists to answer one question early: does this hold up? A headline census over 500 real headlines then sets the clamps and type sizes from measured numbers rather than placeholder copy. Everything downstream is designed against what that shows.

The four constraints

01

Unlimited sources, $20 a month

Collection is nearly free; only AI scales with volume. The AI half is budget-governed, so adding a source costs nothing.

02

~510 summaries from ~7,900

That is 6%. The other 94% must read as complete, not degraded. The compact card is the normal card.

03

Every section fed daily

AI & ML would eat the whole budget on volume. A floor per section, then a merit pool.

04

Nobody edits the front page

So the rules that fill it must be good, inspectable, and overridable by hand in three seconds.

Tech stack

All Cloudflare. One platform, no servers, and the pieces the portal grew into — images and backups — are native to it.

Rejected, and why. Postgres + pgvector — better archive ceiling and real FTS, but adds a vendor, connection pooling and ~$20/mo the AI budget needs more. A VPS — no platform limits, but you own uptime and patching. Next.js on Vercel — good DX, worse cron, and a $20/mo Pro tier for what Workers does at $5. Revisit Postgres only if D1's 10 GB ceiling actually bites, which retention should prevent for three years.

The phases

00

Scaffold

An empty but deployed Worker with a real database behind it.
0.5 DAYBLOCKS EVERYTHING
  1. Upgrade to Workers Paid. Hard prerequisite.
  2. Create D1, KV, an R2 bucket, the collect and enrich queues, and a Vectorize index (bge-small, 384 dims).
  3. migrations/0001_init.sql — full schema including articles_fts and its sync triggers. Write them now; backfilling FTS later is miserable.
  4. GET /health across D1, KV, R2, Vectorize and last run per stage.
  5. sources.seed.json — ~200 feeds across ten sections. Seed, deploy.
Done when

/health is green in production and sources counts ~200.

01

Collector, ending in a reality check

The feed fills itself — and the layout meets real headlines.
4 DAYSBLOCKS P2COSTS $0
Fetching
  1. Tiered scheduler — cron every minute picks sources whose poll is due. A 2–5 min, B 15 min, C hourly, D 6–24 h; intervals adapt.
  2. Conditional GET — a 304 costs almost nothing. Content-hash fallback for feeds with no validators.
  3. WebSub at POST /websub — the only genuinely real-time path.
  4. Politeness — per-domain floor, honour Retry-After, hard 429 backoff, real User-Agent with a contact URL.
Parsing
  1. Parsers to one shape: RSS 2.0, Atom, JSON Feed. Adapters for HN Algolia, Reddit, GitHub releases, arXiv, Google News, Lobste.rs.
  2. Bulk OPML import — what makes “unlimited sources” true rather than aspirational.
  3. Headline normalisation on ingest — strip appended outlet names, collapse ALL-CAPS to sentence case, straighten quotes, drop trailing ellipses, lift EXCLUSIVE — prefixes into a badge. Store raw and normalised.
  4. Rule-based section and topic classification plus a heuristic score at insert. No AI, so every article is usable the moment it lands.
The reality check — the point of the phase
  1. GET /raw — composition slots filled with live data. No CSS.
  2. Headline census over 500 real headlines: length distribution, longest token, how many arrive ALL-CAPS, how many carry the outlet name, how many have no thumbnail.
  3. Feed the census back into the design — lead clamp, row clamp and thumbnail fallback set from measured numbers.
Done when

24 hours unattended yields ~7,900 rows each with a section and a score; re-running adds zero; and /raw shows a front page you can read, built from real headlines, with the census written down.

Watch out for

Feeds that lie about encoding, use six date formats, or omit GUIDs. Wrap every source so one bad feed cannot fail a run.

02

Cluster

Eight outlets on one story become one entry, with an arrival timeline.
2 DAYSBLOCKS P3COSTS ~$0

Before summarizing, because we summarize clusters rather than articles. That ordering is most of why $20 works.

  1. Title-trigram similarity in a 48-hour window flags candidates. Pure SQL.
  2. Embeddings only for the ambiguous ones — ~600–800/day, inside the free allocation. Vector search resolves them. No LLM call anywhere in this phase.
  3. Cluster records — primary by source weight and completeness; members keep their own headlines and links.
  4. Arrival timeline per cluster: first-seen plus per-source offsets. This is what the story page renders.
  5. Corroboration and velocity — sources per hour, not just total.
Done when

Clusters average >1.8 members on genuinely duplicated stories, 30 spot-checked show no false merges, and the timeline reads correctly.

Watch out for

Over-merging. Two Nvidia announcements on one day are not one story. A missed merge is an annoyance; a false merge hides news.

03

Summarizer

~510 cluster summaries a day, every section fed, under a hard cap.
2.5 DAYSTHE WHOLE BUDGET
  1. Per-section allocation — a floor of ~40 per section per day, then a merit pool of ~110 by your read rate, recomputed nightly.
  2. Merit selection within a section: corroboration, velocity, score, recency. A cron every 10 minutes enqueues what the allocation affords.
  3. Summarize the cluster. One call covers every member.
  4. Cost discipline together: system prompt above 1,024 tokens so caching engages; max_tokens ~130; 85% Batch API.
  5. Spend ledger in KV. At the cap, selection stops; the fast path is untouched.
Done when

~510 summaries daily, every section clears its floor, a week of ledger under $0.45/day. Verify cache_read_input_tokens > 0.

Watch out for

Anything in a summary not in the source excerpt — store the excerpt each came from. Batch results arrive out of order; key by custom_id.

04

Editorial engine

What makes this a portal rather than a ranked list.
2.5 DAYSNEW PHASE
  1. The five lead gates — 3+ independent sources; has a summary; under 6 hours or still gaining sources; not the same section as yesterday's lead unless it wins by 25%; not muted by preferences.
  2. Manual pin — one click pins any story as lead for 12 hours, overriding all five, with a visible marker that the page is not running on rules.
  3. Slot filler — lead → 2 hero secondaries → 4 across → 10 section blocks × 5 → 12 latest → 4 most covered. Each slot removes its picks from the pool below. No story appears twice.
  4. Empty states — no story clears the gates → lead collapses and four-across promotes; quiet section shows what it has and says so; no image → thumbnail dropped, not greyed.
  5. Image pipeline — extract media:thumbnail/og:image, validate dimensions and content-type, hotlink with loading="lazy", cache to R2 only for sources that block hotlinking.
  6. Composition cache — recompose every 10 minutes into KV, so a page view is one KV read rather than fifteen D1 queries.
Done when

/api/frontpage.json returns 73 stories with no duplicates, every slot filled or correctly collapsed, and the pin works.

06

Slack

The digest arrives without you touching anything.
1.5 DAYS
  1. Manifest and a single bot token — your own workspace, no OAuth flow.
  2. v0 HMAC over the raw body, 5-minute replay window. Read the body before any JSON parsing or the HMAC never matches.
  3. 3-second ack — respond immediately, work in ctx.waitUntil(), post via response_url.
  4. Block Kit digest by section; breaking pushes to a separate channel at 3+ sources within an hour.
07

Email

The digest arrives in your inbox.
1 DAYNEEDS A DOMAIN
  1. Domain onboarded, SPF and DKIM, send_email binding.
  2. Table layout, inline styles, plain-text alternative. Email clients are not browsers.
  3. Subscriber table, double opt-in, one-click unsubscribe.
08

Agent loop

Ranking, budget and lead contention shift toward what you read.
2 DAYSNEEDS P5 OR P6
  1. Feedback aggregated per source, section and topic.
  2. Weekly preference pass rewrites a preferences document that feeds the scoring prompt, shifts the per-section budget, and can mute a topic out of lead contention.
  3. Source weights move within 0.3–2.0 bounds. Weekly gap sweep proposes new sources as Slack approve/reject buttons. Self-repair after three failures.
Watch out for

Feedback loops eat themselves. Reserve 2 slots per section for high-scoring stories your preferences do not favour, or the portal narrows to nothing.

09

Hardening

It survives you ignoring it for a month.
2 DAYS
  1. Nightly D1 export to R2 with one verified restore.
  2. Unit tests on parsers, canonicalizer, normaliser, classifier, scorer and slot filler — the six places a silent bug corrupts data.
  3. Retention: keep summaries and metadata, drop raw excerpts after 90 days. D1 caps at 10 GB and 7,900/day reaches it in about three years otherwise.
  4. Rate limiting, error budget on /health, and a runbook.
